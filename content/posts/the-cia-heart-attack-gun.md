I heard about this on a Joe Rogan episode. I can't remember which one, and I couldn't find it when I looked. The idea sounded like pure conspiracy: a gun that gives people heart attacks. Then I looked it up and the CIA really did build one.

## What I found

In 1975, the Church Committee was investigating the CIA in Congress. CIA Director William Colby showed the committee a weapon built to kill without leaving evidence, and he called it a "nondiscernible microbioinoculator." It was a modified pistol that fired a tiny dart. The dart carried a shellfish toxin, entered through clothing, and left a small red dot on the skin. The target would go down looking like a heart attack.

## Things worth knowing

- It was shown to Congress in public, which is why it's more than a rumor.
- The toxin was frozen so the dart would dissolve in the body and leave nothing behind.
- Whether it was ever used on anyone is disputed. I found sources arguing both ways, and nothing that settles it.
- The journalist Jack Anderson is often named as someone who was reportedly considered as a target. Treat that as a claim, not a fact.

## Do I think it's real?

Yes. The 1975 version is documented, and that's only what we know about. My assumption is that the government is somewhere between 10 and 30 years ahead of anything we see, so a better version isn't a stretch.

Think about the Venezuelan president getting grabbed. They went in, took him quickly, and by the time anyone noticed it was over. I also remember reports of people losing control of their bowels and bleeding from the eyes, though I haven't tracked down a source for that, so take it as hearsay. Even so, it makes me think there's tech that rarely gets used and gets saved for the missions where nobody can know it exists.

Maybe I'm wrong. I'd still bet there's more in the vault than a dart gun from the 70s.
