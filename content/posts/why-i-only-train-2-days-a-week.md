I've been doing this for 15 years. Two days a week in the gym. That's it.

Sounds lazy, right? It's not. I learned this from Alex Leonidas, old school fitness YouTuber who's been around forever. I watched him back in the day, bought his program called Naturally Enhanced, and it just clicked. He taught people how to train twice a week with two extra days of GPP (General Physical Preparedness) and I've basically been running that ever since.

Yeah, I've taken breaks and tried other programs here and there. Tried some random stuff for a few months. But I always come back to this. It works.

Here's the thing: it's simple but it's not easy. You're still putting in serious work.

## The Split

- **Heavy Day:** Sets of 5 to 10 reps, going for PRs
- **Volume Day:** Sets of 10 to 25 reps, sometimes even 100 to 200 if you're just pumping blood into the muscles for recovery

It sounds basic, but the magic is in the execution.

## The Game Changer: Switch It Up Every Week

You don't hammer the same movement every time. Your joints and connective tissue get a break while your muscles are still getting worked.

Here's an example chest rotation:

- Week 1: Bench press
- Week 2: Dumbbell press
- Week 3: Push-ups
- Week 4: Dips

Still hitting chest, different angle every time. Your body recovers better because you're not absolutely destroying it with the same exercise over and over.

## Why This Actually Works (For Natural Lifters)

I'm a natural lifter. No drugs, no PEDs. As a natural, your recovery is way slower than someone juicing. You need about one to two days to recover from a hard workout. So this schedule just makes sense.

When you go into the gym, you're going in 100%. You're not beat up from training three days ago. You can actually give full effort. I train to failure on pretty much every set because I have the recovery to back it up.

This wouldn't work if you were training multiple days a week. You'd be destroying yourself without enough time to bounce back.

## The Bottom Line

Train heavy one day, train volume one day, vary your exercises, and let your body actually recover between sessions.
